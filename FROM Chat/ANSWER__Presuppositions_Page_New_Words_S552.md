## 2. New: Standing on the Shoulders of Giants

- **Heading:** Standing on the Shoulders of Giants
- **Lead with Bar:** The thinkers whose beliefs about people shape how every Achology course is taught. (82 characters, 13 words)

One card per thinker: the name, then the words, then the link.

**Richard Bandler and John Grinder.** Link: Neuro-Linguistic Programming: What NLP Is and How It Works (/learn/motivation/articles/neuro-linguistic-programming/)
The co-founders of neuro-linguistic programming (NLP) drew up its first list of presuppositions about 50 or 60 years ago. They built it by modelling the work of Virginia Satir, Fritz Perls and Milton Erickson. The presuppositions are working assumptions, not proven facts, and one of them is that the map is not the territory. Achology teaches them early in its NLP courses, and asks you to test each one against your own experience.

**Carl Rogers.** Link: Who Founded Humanistic Psychology? Maslow, Rogers and May (/learn/psychology/articles/the-origins-of-humanistic-psychology/)
Rogers believed that every person carries a pull towards growth, which he called the actualising tendency, and that it works once the right conditions are in place. That belief sits under Achology's first presupposition, that every human being can grow and mature.

**Albert Ellis.** Link: Who Was Albert Ellis? The Psychologist Who Created REBT (/learn/psychology/articles/learn-about-the-psychologist-dr-albert-ellis/)
Ellis argued that we're upset less by events than by the beliefs we form about them, and built rational emotive behaviour therapy (REBT) on that idea. His ABC model puts the belief between what happens and how you feel. Achology's third, fourth and fifth presuppositions stand on the same ground: check the thought, and the feeling becomes yours to manage.

**Viktor Frankl.** Link: What Does Man's Search for Meaning Teach About Purpose? (/learn/psychology/articles/finding-lifes-purpose-with-viktor-frankls-mans-search-for-meaning/)
Frankl was a psychiatrist who lived through the concentration camps. In Man's Search for Meaning he wrote that meaning can be found even in the worst conditions, and that the choice of how to respond can't be taken from you. Achology agrees that the work of meaning is yours, and our seventh presupposition says that personal growth always serves a purpose.

**Abraham Maslow.** Link: the same humanistic psychology article as Rogers.
Maslow argued that you learn about health by studying people who flourish, not only people who struggle. Late in his life he placed a purpose beyond the self above every other human need. Achology's seventh presupposition takes that seriously: feeling better is welcome, but it's never the whole reason to grow.

**Stance paragraph, after the cards:**
None of these ideas began with us, and we don't claim them. What we do is say which ones we stand on. Most courses, in our experience, never publish what they believe about people, though every course stands somewhere. Achology names its seven presuppositions, and they shape how every course is taught.

## 3. What Presuppositions Are

- **Heading:** What Presuppositions Are, and Why They Matter
- **Lead with Bar:** Beliefs about yourself, other people and the world, and what each of them changes. (82 characters, 14 words)

**Opening line, above the three parts:**
A presupposition is something you take to be true before you start. Most are never said out loud, and most were never chosen. They come in three kinds.

**About Yourself**
Believe you're no good with numbers, and you stop trying long before the sum gets hard. Believe you can learn, and the same sum becomes something to work at. Nothing about the sum has changed. What changed is what you took to be true about yourself before you began.

**About Other People**
A friend reads your message and doesn't reply for two days. If you take people to be mostly kind, you guess she's busy. If you take them to be careless with you, you decide she's rude, and you answer her coolly when she does write. The silence is the same in both cases.

**About the World**
The car repair costs twice what you expected. If you believe the world is against you, it's one more proof, and the whole week goes sour. If you believe setbacks come to everyone, it's a bad bill, and you start working out how to pay it.

**Closing paragraph, under the three parts:**
What we presuppose about ourselves, other people and the world shapes how we relate to everyone we meet. People feel it, and they tend to answer in kind. The friend you answered coolly answers coolly too, and the belief that she was rude starts to look like proof. That's why Achology names its own.

The three examples come from our own articles: the maths label (The Seven Beliefs Achology Is Built On), the unanswered message (Who Was Albert Ellis?) and the car repair estimate (Wisdom Begins with Accurate Thinking).
