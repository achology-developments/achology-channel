**Viktor Frankl** (49 words, 289 characters)
Frankl was a psychiatrist who lived through the concentration camps. In Man's Search for Meaning, he wrote that meaning can be found even in the worst conditions, and that how you respond can't be taken from you. Our seventh presupposition agrees that the work of finding meaning is yours.

**Carl Rogers** (48 words, 304 characters)
Rogers believed every person carries the resources to grow, and that the right relationship releases them. In 1957 he set out what it needs, among them warm acceptance with no strings and empathic understanding. This sits under Achology's first presupposition, that every human being can grow and mature.

- Added from its article: the 1957 conditions, in the article's own words ("warm acceptance with no strings and empathic understanding").

**Abraham Maslow** (50 words, 302 characters)
Maslow argued that you learn about health by studying people who flourish, not only people who struggle. Late in life he found that the most mature people he studied all served a cause outside themselves. Our seventh presupposition agrees: feeling better is welcome, but never the whole reason to grow.

**Albert Ellis** (50 words, 296 characters)
Ellis argued that we're upset less by events than by the beliefs we form about them, and built rational emotive behaviour therapy (REBT) on that idea. His ABC model puts the belief between what happens and how you feel. Achology's third, fourth and fifth presuppositions stand on the same ground.

**Richard Bandler and John Grinder**, the wide card (55 words, 344 characters)
The co-founders of neuro-linguistic programming (NLP) drew up its first presuppositions about 50 or 60 years ago, modelling Virginia Satir, Fritz Perls and Milton Erickson. They're working assumptions, not proven facts, and one is that the map is not the territory. Achology teaches them early in its NLP courses, to test against your own life.
